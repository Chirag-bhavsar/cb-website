UX Case Study — Mobile App

Connecting people in need with local charities.

Maslow surfaces nearby charities the moment someone indicates what kind of help they need — food, shelter, clothing — and gives charities a direct channel to the people looking for them.

Role

End-to-End UX/UI Design

Platform

iOS, mobile-first

Deliverables

IA, Wireframes, Design System, Hi-Fi UI

Typeface

Lato

The Brief

Help exists nearby. Nobody can find it.

The Problem

People who need help rarely know which local organization can provide it. A search for "food assistance near me" turns into a maze of hotlines, outdated flyers, and word of mouth — slow and often humbling at the exact moment someone needs speed and dignity.

  • No single place to see which charities serve a given need nearby
  • Hotlines and directories are slow, outdated, or incomplete
  • Charities have no reliable channel to reach new people
  • No easy way to promote or manage in-person events
  • Manual, paper-based check-in at events

The Solution

Maslow acts as a directory, locator, and engagement layer between the two groups — charities register the services they offer and where, and the app routes a user straight to the nearest source of help.

  • Search by service type, resolved to nearby charities on a map or list
  • Charities register services, locations, and hosted events
  • Users browse and RSVP to charity-hosted events
  • QR check-in at events replaces paper sign-in sheets
  • Profile & notifications keep the relationship going after day one
Who We Designed For

Two sides of the same search.

Maslow is a two-sided product — someone looking for help, and an organization trying to be found. Every flow decision traced back to one of these two people.

MT

Maria, 34

Seeking help · first-time user

"I don't want to make five phone calls to find out where to bring my kids for a meal tonight."

Goals

  • Find verified help in minutes, not hours
  • See exactly where and when it's available
  • Avoid explaining her situation over and over

Frustrations

  • Doesn't know which local orgs serve her area
  • Hotlines have long waits, unclear hours
  • Feels exposed asking for help in public
DP

Diane, 52

Charity program coordinator

"We show up every week — the problem is nobody outside our regulars knows we're here."

Goals

  • List services and hours in one place
  • Get discovered by people nearby, not just regulars
  • Run events and track who actually shows up

Frustrations

  • No shared channel to reach new visitors
  • Manual check-in and paper sign-ins at events
  • Word-of-mouth doesn't scale past her block

Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Testing the flow before the finish.

Every screen in the core journey was blocked out in grayscale first, with annotations documenting intended behavior before a single color or icon was decided.

Annotated behavior, straight from the wireframes: "Allow user to open scanner when they arrive at event." · "Event location will be shown on map." · "Notify users their profile is incomplete." These notes kept the flow's logic agreed upon before visual design began.
Visual Design System

Calm, trustworthy, never clinical.

The palette pairs a trustworthy teal — associated with care and support — with a warm coral accent, keeping the interface approachable for users who may be in a vulnerable moment.

Reflection

Structure first, detail second, identity last.

Information architecture and user flow defined where things live. Card sorting validated how users group them. Wireframes tested how the flow feels. The visual design system gave the product a warm, trustworthy identity.

The result is a clear, low-friction path connecting someone in need directly to the charity best equipped to help them — and a direct channel for charities to reach the people they exist to serve.
