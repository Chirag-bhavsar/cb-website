01

Overview

For the past four years, I've been part of a small design team redesigning a large enterprise financial platform used to manage accounts, collections, payments, legal processes, and financial operations.

The product contains many complex workflows, business rules, financial calculations, integrations, and data-heavy screens. My role has been to take requirements from Jira stories and business discussions, understand the workflow, and turn them into simple, consistent and scalable user experiences.

The goal wasn't to remove complexity — it was to make complexity easier to understand and manage.
02

The Challenge

The existing application had grown over time and contained many complex workflows. Users often had to work with:

  • Large amounts of financial data
  • Complex forms
  • Multiple statuses
  • Business rules
  • Conditional fields
  • Contextual actions
  • Payment and settlement information
  • Legal workflows
  • Third-party integrations

The challenge was to redesign these experiences without disrupting important business processes.

03

My Role

I worked as part of a small design team consisting of a Design Manager and two UI/UX Designers. I contributed to the redesign of 25+ screens and workflows, working from Jira stories through design and developer handoff.

My work included:

  • Understanding business requirements
  • Mapping user workflows
  • Information architecture
  • Wireframing
  • UI/UX design
  • Interaction design
  • Form and table design
  • Designing validation and error states
  • Creating reusable components
  • Developer collaboration
  • Design QA

The project follows an Agile methodology, so designs continuously evolve based on new requirements, feedback and product changes.

04

Designing for Complexity

One of the biggest UX challenges was dealing with the amount of information users need to work with. Instead of trying to hide information, I focused on:

Organize → Prioritize → Simplify → Guide
  • Clear information hierarchy
  • Logical grouping
  • Tabs and sections
  • Progressive disclosure
  • Contextual actions
  • Clear status indicators
  • Consistent forms and tables

Payments

Payments contains some of the most business-critical workflows in the platform. I worked on areas including:

  • Client payment posting
  • Payment history
  • Refund checks
  • Payment arrangements
  • Settlement offers
  • Financial transactions

One challenge was presenting complex financial information without overwhelming the user. For settlement offers, for example, users may need to understand offer status, start and expiration dates, payment options, discounted amount, periodic payments, and payment terms. I used structured layouts and clear status communication to make this information easier to scan.

Account Management

Manual account creation was another complex workflow I redesigned. Creating an account can involve multiple sets of information — client and contract, customer details, guarantor information, address, phone, email, and account information.

Rather than presenting everything as one long form, I organized the information into logical sections. This helped users focus on the information relevant to the current step.

Designing Better Forms

Forms were an important part of the redesign. I worked on required fields, validation, conditional fields, error messages, expandable sections, pre-populated information, and confirmation dialogs. The intention was to give users immediate feedback and make errors easier to understand and correct.

08

Complex Business Rules

A significant part of the work involved translating business rules into usable interactions. For example, changing a client or contract could affect account information — certain actions could only be performed under specific conditions.

Instead of leaving these rules invisible, I used interface patterns such as:

  • Confirmation dialogs
  • Conditional fields
  • Disabled states
  • Informational messages
  • Validation
  • Contextual actions

This helped users understand why something was happening and what they needed to do next.

My Design Process

The project follows an Agile workflow: Jira Story → Understand → Explore → Design → Review → Refine → Handoff.

01

Understand

I start with the Jira story and business requirements provided by Product/BA.

02

Explore

I understand the existing workflow, dependencies, business rules and user actions.

03

Design

I explore the information structure and create wireframes before moving to high-fidelity UI.

04

Review

I collaborate with the Design Manager, Product, BA and development team.

05

Refine

I incorporate feedback and resolve usability or business-rule issues.

06

Handoff

The final design is prepared for development and discussed with developers to ensure the intended experience is implemented correctly.

12

What I Learned

Enterprise UX is less about making things simple and more about making complexity understandable.

Working with financial data, business rules, legal processes and integrations taught me to look beyond individual screens and understand the entire workflow behind them.

It also reinforced the importance of consistency. A well-designed component or interaction pattern can solve the same problem across many different parts of a large product.
